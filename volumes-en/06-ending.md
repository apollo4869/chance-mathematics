An infinite record can acquire a stable average without giving every record the same answer. The hidden-parameter model showed that more observations can remove sampling fluctuations while retaining information about the environment to which this record belongs. The conditional expectation in the Birkhoff limit expresses precisely this distinction.

Stability therefore cannot replace all the hypotheses. A two-point alternating system can be ergodic while retaining the parity of time; a unique invariant probability need not attract every starting state. Forgetting required an additional coupling or regeneration mechanism that actually brings future laws together. Averaging, shifting and forgetting answer different questions.

Changing the allowed transformations from time shifts to arbitrary finite relabellings changed the information that remained. Exchangeable observations need not be independent: they may keep sharing a random probability. The force of de Finetti representation lies in identifying how dependence can be organised, rather than eliminating it. Empirical measures make that common probability recoverable from the infinite law instead of merely postulating it at the outset.

Prediction rules then reversed the direction. Certain updates construct an exchangeable infinite record, but normalising each next step is insufficient: joint probabilities must also agree when the observation order changes. The compactness restriction in group averaging, and the counterexample with a data-selected transformation, make a related distinction. A fixed transformation preserving a law does not license every rule for observing and selecting transformations. What may change, and which information may select the change, must be specified.

Relation arrays carried the distinction into shared information. Relations containing the same person may reuse a random input, while disjoint groups may still share a global environment. Representation identifies these levels without giving latent variables unique names. An observable law and a particular coordinate system used to generate it remain different objects.

Finally, transitivity allowed class masses to emerge from an infinite classification. Positive-frequency paintbox blocks and dust must both be retained: individual singletons have zero frequency, yet together can occupy a positive fraction. Relabelling removes class names, not classification. Making classes merge requires further clocks and rates. The meeting of an Ewens sampling law with a Kingman genealogy required a mutation mechanism and a recursion proof, not just their shared exchangeability.

Symmetry has thus become a more precise mathematical requirement. It neither guarantees the disappearance of dependence nor supplies dynamics by itself. It specifies which probability information must survive a chosen class of changes. Different allowed changes can therefore reveal different random structures.

Our objects have so far carried observers or members that can be relabelled. If an experiment instead produces actual locations in space, those locations cannot simply be renamed. How many points occupy a window, where they lie, and whether they coincide become parts of one random outcome. The next difficulty is to express the whole collection in a form that preserves spatial information while permitting measurable observations and limits.
