# T-RADON-SPACE: The Space of Nonnegative Radon Measures

Return to [Chapter 49](../chapters/49-random-measures.md#mass-polish). E is a locally compact second-countable metric space. We first construct deterministic \(\mathcal M_+(E)\), then its random elements. We use [T-RIESZ](t-riesz.md), Chapters 5–6, and the compact exhaustion and test families actually constructed in Chapter 48, without future Palm theory.

## 1. Countable Tests with Controlled Supports

<a id="radon-tests"></a>

Choose \(K_m\subset\operatorname{int}K_{m+1}\), \(\bigcup_mK_m=E\), and cutoffs \(0\le\chi_m\le1\), \(\chi_m=1\) on \(K_m\), \(\operatorname{supp}\chi_m\subset K_{m+1}\). Enlarging the exhaustion and combining finitely many cutoffs permits increasing χₘ. Finite nets, distance hats, and finite partitions of unity give countable uniform approximation families on compact windows: take centers from a countable dense set, rational radii and values, and multiply by cutoffs. Every \(f\in C_c\) is uniformly approximated by members supported in one fixed larger K. For nonnegative f choose nonnegative approximants, and handle signed tests through their positive and negative parts.

Include χₘ and nonnegative rational combinations in the nonnegative family \((f_j)\), and use its rational linear span V for linear relations. Riesz uniqueness makes these coordinates separating. If \(\mu_nf_j\to\mu f_j\), then \(\sup_n\mu_n(K_m)\le\sup_n\mu_n\chi_m<\infty\). For f supported in K and an approximant g, the error is at most \(\|f-g\|_\infty(\mu_n(K)+\mu(K))\). Fix the approximation, pass n to the limit, and then reduce the approximation error. This gives convergence for all \(C_c\) tests.

## 2. Positive Functional Limits and Relative Compactness

<a id="radon-compact"></a>

Suppose \(\sup_n\mu_n(K_m)<\infty\) for each m. Every coordinate is bounded. Successive extraction and a diagonal subsequence make all V coordinates converge. Define \(Lq=\lim_n\mu_nq\) on V. It is rational-linear, positive for q≥0, and obeys \(|Lq|\le a_K\|q\|_\infty\) on each fixed support K. Approximation within a common support defines Lf for every real \(C_c\) function. The bound makes the limit exist independently of the approximating sequence. To compare two choices of support, use one larger compact containing both. Approximation of real coefficients by rationals gives real linearity.

Positivity also survives: choose nonnegative approximants, or add δ times a cutoff equal to one when the approximation error is δ, then let δ vanish. T-RIESZ gives a unique nonnegative Radon measure μ. The same error bound gives convergence to μ for every compactly supported test along the subsequence. A locally mass-bounded family is therefore sequentially relatively compact, hence has compact closure in the metric space. Conversely, on a compact closure each continuous map μ↦μχₘ has a finite upper bound, which bounds local masses.

## 3. Completeness, Separability, and the Integer Subspace

<a id="radon-polish"></a>

\[ d_v(\mu,\nu)=\sum_j2^{-j}(1\wedge|\mu f_j-\nu f_j|). \]

This metric induces the vague topology. Every coordinate of a \(d_v\)-Cauchy sequence is real Cauchy; cutoff coordinates in particular are bounded. The preceding section gives a convergent subsequence, and the Cauchy property gives convergence of the whole sequence. Thus this specific metric is complete. Separability requires rational weights, not integer configurations. Given finitely many tests and δ>0, restrict μ to a compact K containing their supports. Partition K into finitely many Borel cells small enough for the common uniform-continuity scale of the tests, and move each cell’s mass to a nearby fixed point of a countable dense set. The reading error is at most δμ(K). Approximate the finitely many masses by rationals. The resulting countable family of finite rational atomic measures is dense in the metric.

Closedness of \(\mathcal M_p\) uses the continuity-ball proof in [T-POINT-MEASURES](t-point-measures.md#point-space-compact). For a Radon limit, local finiteness makes all but countably many radii have zero-mass spheres. Continuous cutoffs make the mass of each such ball an integer limit. Shrinking balls gives integer singleton masses; every nonatomic point has a zero-mass neighborhood, and a countable base eliminates any remainder. The subspace is closed and Polish. Density of finite weighted atoms in \(\mathcal M_+\) does not imply density of integer atoms.

## 4. Evaluations, Common Null Sets, and Random Kernels

<a id="radon-evaluation"></a>

Let \(\mathcal E\) be generated by all evaluations \(\mu\mapsto\mu(A)\). Nonnegative Borel integrals are measurable by simple approximation and MCT, so continuous-test coordinates are \(\mathcal E\)-measurable. Separability makes open sets countable unions of basic opens, yielding \(\mathcal B_v\subset\mathcal E\). Conversely, increasing \(C_c^+\) functions approximate an open set U, making μ(U) Borel. In a fixed relatively compact open V, the sets A⊂V with measurable evaluation contain relative open sets, permit relative complements because μ(V) is finite, and are closed under disjoint countable unions. The π–λ theorem gives every Borel evaluation within V, and a countable open exhaustion gives every evaluation on E. Hence \(\mathcal E=\mathcal B_v=\sigma\{\mu f_j:j\ge1\}\).

For a map already taking Radon values in every realization, measurable countable readings suffice. If only random linear readings are given, one additionally needs almost-sure rational linearity, positivity, and finite local bounds on the countable V. There are only countably many relations. Remove their combined null set and repeat the extension and Riesz construction realization by realization, assigning zero measure on the exceptional set. The countable coordinates are measurable, so the result is a random measure. Uniqueness of versions likewise first uses a common null set for countable coordinates and then extends to all Borel sets. Separate almost-sure statements for each f do not establish a common version by themselves.

In particular, \(P(E)=\{\mu:\mu(E)=1\}\) is Borel and Polish. Since \(\mu(E)=\sup_m\mu\chi_m\), the set \(\{\mu(E)\le1\}\) is closed, and \(\{\mu(E)\ge1\}=\bigcap_k\bigcup_m\{\mu\chi_m>1-1/k\}\) is Gδ. Closed sets in metric spaces are Gδ too, so P(E) is a Gδ subset of a Polish space. Its weak topology equals the vague subspace topology, as proved in the next companion. The Gδ completeness fact also has a direct construction: for G=∩Uⱼ in a complete ambient space, embed x as (x,(1/dist(x,Uⱼᶜ))ⱼ), taking a zero coordinate when Uⱼ is the whole space. The graph is closed in the product of the ambient space and real sequences, hence inherits a complete metric with the original topology on G. A countable base gives separability.
