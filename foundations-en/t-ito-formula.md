# T-ITO-FORMULA: Change of Variables and Integration Conventions for Continuous Paths

This supplement proves the continuous-semimartingale formulas used in Chapter 32, without general SDE existence, jump integration or future nonsmooth formulas. Its basis is the continuous-local-martingale integral, ucp convergence of deterministic-grid square sums, covariation and stopping identities constructed in [T-ITO-BRACKET](t-ito-bracket.md). Usual conditions are in force; local martingale and finite-variation parts start at zero. Ucp means uniform convergence in probability on every bounded interval. These are complete working proofs, with author and independent final mathematical review still open.

## 1. Why Weighted Quadratic Sums Converge

<a id="t-ito-weighted"></a>

Let M be a continuous local martingale and A=[M]. Fix T and deterministic grids \(\pi_n\) with mesh tending to zero, not necessarily nested. Let \(\widehat Q_n\) add a square only when its interval is completed, with zero contribution from the current interval. It is increasing. Its uniform difference from the sum \(Q_n\) that includes the current partial interval is at most \(\omega_M(|\pi_n|)^2\). Thus \(\widehat Q_n\) converges uniformly in probability to A, and \(\widehat Q_n\)(T) is tight. Here ω denotes path oscillation on the fixed compact interval. These facts come from Chapter 31, not from the formula being proved.

For an arbitrary continuous random weight g, the assertion below is a pathwise Stieltjes limit; g need not be adapted. First replace g at each left endpoint by its right-endpoint value. The uniform error is at most \(\omega_g(|\pi_n|)\widehat Q_n(T)\), tending to zero in probability. For a fixed coarse grid \(u_j\), form the step function \(g^k\) using g(\(u_j\)). For each such function the integral difference is at most 2Σ\_j|g(\(u_j\))|‖\(\widehat Q_n\)−A‖∞, hence tends to zero in probability. Approximation errors for g versus \(g^k\) satisfy the following bound. Random finite coefficients can first be restricted to arbitrarily high-probability events; expectation bounds are unnecessary.

\[ \sup_{t\le T}\left|\int_0^t(g-g^k)\,d\widehat Q_n\right|+\sup_{t\le T}\left|\int_0^t(g-g^k)\,dA\right|\le\omega_g(|u^k|)\{\widehat Q_n(T)+A_T\}. \]

First let n tend to infinity, then refine the coarse grid. Tightness and pathwise uniform continuity control the displayed bound. The unfinished interval contributes at most ‖g‖∞\(\omega_M(|\pi_n|)^2\). This proves the main text’s ucp weighted-square limit, not merely terminal convergence. Polarization expresses cross sums for M,N through squares of M+N and M−N, giving the limit ∫g d[M,N] for continuous weights. The variation of the signed limiting measure is controlled by the Stieltjes Cauchy–Schwarz inequality proved in Chapter 31.

If V is continuous finite variation, Σ(ΔV)²≤\(\omega_V(|\pi_n|)\operatorname{Var}(V)\) tends to zero pathwise, uniformly in the terminal time. Cross sums are bounded by √(ΣΔM² ΣΔV²) by finite-sum Cauchy–Schwarz, and tend to zero ucp. Hence X=X0+M+V has [X]=[M]; vector cross sums work the same way, and Σ‖ΔX‖² is tight on bounded intervals. These are the common controls needed for the remainders. Return to [Section 32.3](../chapters/32-ito-formula.md#ito-weighted-variation).

## 2. Left Sums, Finite-Variation Integration and Localization

<a id="t-ito-localization"></a>

Let X be a d-dimensional continuous adapted semimartingale, X=X0+M+V, with almost surely finite F0-measurable X0, continuous local martingale M and continuous adapted finite-variation V. For continuous adapted H define ∫H dX=∫H dM+∫H dV, using Chapter 31’s local integral and pathwise Lebesgue–Stieltjes integration respectively. Local boundedness on compact intervals makes both terms exist after localization. The variation process of V is continuous and adapted: rational partitions give a countable supremum determining its variation. Measurability of random Stieltjes integration is first verified for predictable rectangles, then extended by the functional monotone-class theorem, as for the clock measure in Chapter 31.

For explicit localization, first work on \(E_R=\{\|X_0\|\le R\}\)∈F0. Stop when any component of M first reaches absolute value R or the sum of the variations of V’s components first reaches R, intersecting existing localizing times when needed. Continuity prevents overshoots. The stopped M is bounded and square integrable, with \(E([M^\tau]_T)=E((M^\tau_T)^2)\) finite. Stopped X lies in a deterministic compact set; all continuous coefficients and derivatives used can be bounded. Multiply by zero off \(E_R\). Finally let R grow and remove the stopping. Finite variation, continuity and localization ensure that any fixed T is covered with probability tending to one.

For an arbitrary continuous adapted H, also restrict the initial event to \(E_R\cap\{|H_0|\le R\}\), intersect the common stopping time with the first time \(|H_t|\ge R\), and use stopped H with the common stopped processes. This bounds H by a deterministic constant, while the initial events and stops still exhaust every finite interval. Control of X alone was sufficient only when H was a deterministic continuous function of (t,X), bounded on compact sets. The two stopping identities are proved in T-ITO-BRACKET Section 5.

In this bounded setting \(H^n_s=H_{t_i}\) for \(t_i<s\le t_{i+1}\) is simple predictable and converges uniformly pathwise to H. Isometry and Doob’s inequality give the bound below. Its right side is dominated by a constant times \([M]_T\) with finite expectation, so dominated convergence applies. The finite-variation error is at most \(\omega_H(|\pi_n|)\operatorname{Var}(V)_T\), tending to zero pathwise. Here both H and total variation have deterministic bounds, so dominated convergence also gives maximal L² convergence of this term. Thus left sums converge in S² after localization and ucp in general. Sum components for vectors. The last interval is cut at the current t; a future completed increment must not be inserted.

\[ E\sup_{t\le T}\left|\int_0^t(H^n_s-H_s)\,dM_s\right|^2\le4E\int_0^T|H^n_s-H_s|^2\,d[M]_s\longrightarrow0. \]

## 3. Completing the One- and Multidimensional Formulas from Taylor’s Identity

<a id="t-ito-general"></a>

Theorem. For X as above and F∈C¹,²([0,∞)×R^d), the following is an identity of indistinguishable continuous processes. C¹,² means one continuous time derivative and two continuous spatial derivatives, jointly continuous with F; neither a second time derivative nor mixed time-space derivatives are required. \(C_{ij}=[M^i,M^j]\) are continuous locally finite-variation processes; no dt density is assumed.

\[ F(t,X_t)-F(0,X_0)=\int_0^t F_s(s,X_s)\,ds+\sum_i\int_0^t F_i(s,X_s)\,dX^i_s+\frac12\sum_{i,j}\int_0^t F_{ij}(s,X_s)\,dC_{ij}(s). \]

Proof. Localize as in Section 2. On each deterministic partition interval, first change time at the new spatial position \(X_{t_{k+1}}\), then change space at the old time t\_k. The time difference is \(\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}F_s(u,X_{t_{k+1}})\,du\). Its total discrepancy from ∫\(F_s\)(u,X\_u)du is at most T times the spatial continuity modulus of \(F_s\) on the compact set, evaluated at \(\omega_X(|\pi|)\), and tends to zero pathwise. For the spatial difference put h=ΔX and use the one-dimensional integral remainder along the line segment. This gives exactly:

\[ F(t,x+h)-F(t,x)=\sum_i F_i(t,x)h_i+\frac12\sum_{i,j}F_{ij}(t,x)h_ih_j+r(t,x,h),\qquad |r(t,x,h)|\le\frac12\omega_{D_x^2F}(|h|)\|h\|^2. \]

The linear sums converge to the stated integrals by Section 2. Each quadratic sum converges to ∫\(F_{ij}\) d\(C_{ij}\) by Section 1 with the continuous random weight \(F_{ij}\)(s,X\_s). The total remainder is uniformly bounded by ½ω\_{D²F}(\(\omega_X(|\pi|)\))Σ‖ΔX‖². Its first factor vanishes pathwise and its second is tight, so the product vanishes ucp. Fixed dimension constants in matrix norms can be absorbed in the modulus. The same estimates cover the current partial interval and hold uniformly for t≤T. Take limits in the finite identity, then remove localization. The continuous difference has zero supremum on each T almost surely; the countable union over integer T gives indistinguishability for all time. No arbitrary-partition pathwise almost sure convergence is asserted.

Taking F independent of time and d=1 gives the one-dimensional C² formula for continuous semimartingales; X=B gives the Brownian version. All quadratic terms involving finite-variation coordinates vanish, recovering the classical formula as a genuine special case. Taking F(x,y)=xy gives the product formula. It can also be proved directly by finite product differences and Section 1; there is no circular dependence.

To identify fluctuations after a transformation, let f,h be C². The formula just proved identifies the local martingale part of f(X) as Σ∫f\_i(X)dM^i, with a continuous finite-variation remainder. Chapter 31 independently proved the mixed bracket transformation for integrals. Hence the following holds; covariation is not being defined backwards through this supplement’s product formula. Return to [Section 32.7](../chapters/32-ito-formula.md#ito-continuous-formula).

\[ [f(X),h(X)]_t=\sum_{i,j}\int_0^t f_i(X_s)h_j(X_s)\,d[M^i,M^j]_s. \]

## 4. The Domain and Chain Rule of Endpoint-Symmetric Integration

<a id="t-ito-stratonovich"></a>

First let H and X be continuous semimartingales. On each interval the endpoint-average sum equals the left sum plus ½ΣΔHΔX, so Sections 1 and 2 give ucp convergence. The finite identity remains exact with the current partial interval included. Defining H∘dX by this limit gives ∫H∘dX=∫H dX+½[H,X]. Arbitrary continuous H has not been admitted to the domain, and the symmetric limit has not been assumed to be a martingale.

A regularity issue matters for the C² chain rule: f∈C² only gives f′∈C¹, and does not by itself establish that f′(X) is a semimartingale. The needed extension has a direct proof. For g∈C¹(R^d), write Δg(X)=Σ\_j \(g_j\)(\(X_{\mathrm{left}}\))ΔX^j+ρ, with |ρ|≤ω\_Dg(\(\omega_X(|\pi|)\))‖ΔX‖. Multiply by ΔX^i and sum. The error is bounded by this modulus times Σ‖ΔX‖², and vanishes ucp. Section 1 gives the formula below. The integral ∫g(X)dX^i already exists since the integrand is continuous adapted and locally bounded. Thus endpoint-average sums converge too. We extend symmetric integration only to this explicitly proved composition domain.

\[ \sum_k\Delta_k g(X)\Delta_k X^i\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ucp}}\sum_j\int_0^\cdot g_j(X_s)\,dC_{ji}(s). \]

For the time-space version take a deterministic function g(t,x), requiring only joint continuity of g and \(D_xg\), without a time derivative. Replace each time difference by \(q_k=g(t_{k+1},X_{t_k})-g(t_k,X_{t_k})\). It is \(\mathcal F_{t_k}\)-measurable and tends locally uniformly to zero. The sum Σ\(q_k\)ΔM^i is a simple predictable integral; bounded localization, isometry and Doob give convergence to zero in maximal mean square. The finite-variation sum is bounded by sup|\(q_k\)|Var(V^i). To compare the original time difference evaluated at \(X_{t_{k+1}}\) with \(q_k\), use the first-order spatial integral formula: the discrepancy is at most \(\omega^{\mathrm{time}}_{D_xg}(|\pi|)\)‖ΔX‖. Its product with ΔX also sums to zero. The current partial interval is separately bounded by a time continuity modulus times \(\omega_X(|\pi|)\). Thus the same cross limit holds for g(t,\(X_t\)).

Here is an explicit uniform estimate for the preceding paragraph. On the localized spatial compact set K let \(\beta(\delta)=\sup_{|u-v|\le\delta,\,x\in K}|g(u,x)-g(v,x)|\), with times in [0,T]; then β(δ) tends to zero. Define the simple integral J with the full-interval coefficients \(q_k\), including the current partial interval. Isometry and Doob give the bound below. Replacing the actual partial-interval time difference by the full \(q_k\) adds at most \(2\beta(\delta)\omega_{M^i}(\delta)\) in maximal absolute value, tending to zero. The finite-variation part is bounded by β(δ) times total variation. The time modulus of D_xg times the quadratic sums controls the remaining replacement error. Thus the result is genuinely ucp, not only convergence at grid endpoints.

\[ E\sup_{t\le T}|J_t|^2\le4\beta(\delta)^2E([M^i]_T)\longrightarrow0. \]

Take g=F\_i for F∈C¹,². The required conditions hold, and the half-covariations in the endpoint-symmetric integrals absorb the Hessian term of Section 3. This proves the C¹,² Stratonovich chain rule without additionally assuming F\_i is a semimartingale. For a given continuous SDE with C¹ coefficients \(\sigma_{kj}\)(x), Chapter 31 first gives [X^k,W^j]=∫\(\sigma_{kj}\)(X)ds. Applying the composition covariation just proved yields ∫\(\sigma_{ij}\)(X)∘dW^j=∫\(\sigma_{ij}\)(X)dW^j+½Σ\_k∫∂\_k\(\sigma_{ij}\)(X)\(\sigma_{kj}\)(X)ds. This converts an existing solution’s equation; it does not prove general existence. Return to [Section 32.12](../chapters/32-ito-formula.md#ito-stratonovich-domain).

## 5. A Temporal Midpoint Need Not Bisect the Quadratic Clock

<a id="t-ito-midpoint"></a>

Here is a full check of the counterexample. Take 0<p<1 with p≠½. Independent ternary digits equal 0 with probability p and 2 with probability 1−p; let \(c_p\) be the distribution function of the resulting Cantor probability \(\nu_p\). An allowed level-n ternary interval has mass q=\(p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\), whose maximum is at most max(p,1−p)^n→0. Hence singletons have zero mass, \(c_p\) is continuous, \(c_p\)(0)=0 and \(c_p\)(1)=1. The clock is constant on middle-third gaps. Set \(M_t=B_{c_p(t)}\), extending \(c_p\)=1 after time 1. Under the usual augmented time-changed filtration proved in Chapter 31, [M]=\(c_p\).

Partition [0,1] into 3^n equal temporal intervals and use each midpoint \(m_i\). Within an interval with mass \(q_i\), the midpoint lies after its left subinterval and before its right subinterval. Thus \(a_i=M_{m_i}-M_{t_i}\) has variance p\(q_i\) and \(b_i=M_{t_{i+1}}-M_{m_i}\) has variance (1−p)\(q_i\). All these centered Gaussian variables are independent; increments on zero-mass intervals vanish. The temporal-midpoint sum therefore exceeds the left sum by \(C_n=\sum_i a_i(a_i+b_i)\). Independence and the Gaussian fourth moment give the exact computation:

\[ EC_n=p\sum_i q_i=p,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(C_n)=\{2p^2+p(1-p)\}\sum_i q_i^2\le2\max_i q_i\longrightarrow0. \]

The left integral has energy E∫M\_s² d\(c_p\)(s)=∫\(c_p\)(s)d\(c_p\)(s)=½, the last equality being the classical Stieltjes chain rule for a continuous distribution function. Its simple left approximation has energy error Σ∫\_{interval i}(\(c_p\)(s)−\(c_p\)(t\_i))d\(c_p\)(s)≤max\_i \(q_i\). Thus the terminal left sums converge in L² to ∫M dM=½(\(M_1^2\)−1). Endpoint-average sums instead equal ½\(M_1^2\) exactly for every n. The temporal-midpoint sums converge at the fixed terminal time in L² to ½(\(M_1^2\)−1)+p, differing from the endpoint-average limit when p≠½. Only this proved terminal L² statement is asserted, not a general theorem for other grids or coefficients. Return to [Section 32.13](../chapters/32-ito-formula.md#ito-midpoint-clock).

## 6. Explicit Linear Equations and Proof Responsibilities

<a id="t-ito-linear-models"></a>

Let M be a continuous local martingale and V continuous adapted finite variation, both starting at zero, and put Z=exp(M+V−½[M]). Section 3 first gives dZ=Z dM+Z dV. Applying it to the negative exponent gives d(Z^{-1})=−Z^{-1}dM−Z^{-1}dV+Z^{-1}d[M]. If continuous adapted X satisfies dX=X dM+X dV, Chapter 31’s integral bracket gives [X,Z^{-1}]=−∫XZ^{-1}d[M]. The product formula cancels all terms, so XZ^{-1}=X0. Every calculation can be bounded at exits of |X|, Z, Z^{-1} and total clocks, then unstopped. Positivity of X has not been assumed and log X has not been used. This constructs these equations and proves their pathwise uniqueness.

Taking M=σB and V=μt gives the geometric Brownian formula in the main text. An arbitrary finite F0-measurable initial value is handled on bounded F0 events. If it is absolutely integrable, the Gaussian exponential moment and independent increments give E[\(X_t\)|F0]=X0 exp(μt); arbitrary nonnegative local martingales are not being declared true martingales. With V=0, general Z is only a nonnegative local martingale: stopped martingales and conditional Fatou first give a supermartingale. Change-of-measure theorems and true-martingale criteria are outside this proof.

The deterministic integrating factor e^{θt} has zero covariation with X, so the product formula gives \(d(e^{\theta t}(X_t-m))=\sigma e^{\theta t}\,dB_t\). The main text’s explicit integral therefore exists and satisfies the equation. The difference of two solutions obeys \(D_t=-\theta\int_0^tD_s\,ds\), hence D=0 pathwise by classical ODE reasoning. Future integrals are Gaussian L² limits of Gaussian linear combinations for simple deterministic integrands, independent of \(\mathcal F_s\): verify joint independence by characteristic functions for simple integrands, then pass to the limit using L² convergence and bounded characteristic functions. Thus the stated conditional means, variances and covariance under a stationary Gaussian initial law have proved foundations rather than a formal solution alone.

The conditional law does not rely on an unproved claim that all SDE solutions are Markov. For bounded Borel f, independence of the future Gaussian integral from \(\mathcal F_s\) directly gives \(E[f(U_t)\mid\mathcal F_s]=K_{t-s}f(U_s)\). Put \(a_u=e^{-\theta u}\) and \(v_u=\sigma^2(1-e^{-2\theta u})/(2\theta)\); \(K_u\) is Gaussian with mean \(m+a_u(x-m)\) and variance \(v_u\). Composing two Gaussian integrals multiplies the centered mean factors and adds the rescaled past variance to the new variance: \(a_{u+v}=a_va_u\) and \(v_{u+v}=a_v^2v_u+v_v\). Hence \(K_uK_v=K_{u+v}\). This establishes OU’s time-homogeneous Markov transitions and its semigroup on bounded Borel observations without Chapter 35’s general SDE theory.

For compactly supported C² f, both Lf and σf′ are bounded. The noise integral is square integrable with mean zero on each finite interval. Boundedness of Lf justifies Fubini and gives Section 32.18’s expectation identity. With deterministic initial x, U_s tends pathwise to x; continuity and boundedness of Lf then give the stated pointwise derivative by dominated convergence. Sections 28.3 and 28.5 distinguish C₀-Feller strong continuity from the supremum-norm generator domain. This paragraph does not replace either requirement by a pointwise derivative. Time-dependent coefficients give an Itô drift L_t, not automatically a one-parameter semigroup. For arbitrary finite but nonintegrable initial values, the means and variances above are moments of the conditional Gaussian kernel; L¹ conditional-expectation identities require the corresponding integrability.
