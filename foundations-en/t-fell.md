# T-FELL: Closed-Set Spaces, Topology, and Measurable Observations

Return to [Chapter 51](../chapters/51-random-closed-sets.md#sets-fell). E is a nonempty locally compact second-countable metric space; closed sets include the empty set. We use the exhaustion and cutoffs of T-RIESZ, not Choquet representation.

## 1. Give Infinity a Location

<a id="fell-compactification"></a>

T-RIESZ supplies continuous φ with compact sublevel sets. The metric \(\rho(x,y)=d(x,y)+|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|\) is compatible, and each closed bounded ball is closed in a compact sublevel set, hence compact. Fix this proper metric. Closed balls with countably dense centers and positive rational radii, and their finite unions, form a countable compact family R; include the empty set. It is union-closed, not necessarily difference-closed. For compact K inside open U choose L∈R with K⊂int L⊂L⊂U: use small balls with closures in U, adjust their centers and radii, then extract a finite interior cover.

Recursively choose Lₙ∈R with K⊂int Lₙ, Lₙ₊₁⊂int Lₙ, and Lₙ⊂{ρ(x,K)≤1/n}; thus Lₙ decreases to K. At each step cover K inside the previous interior intersected with a distance neighborhood. Fix one sequence for each K in R; there are countably many.

For compactification we avoid a new topological black box. Choose countably many [0,1]-valued compactly supported continuous fⱼ separating points, generating the topology, and positive somewhere at every point; cutoffs of a relatively compact countable base and rational thresholds provide them. Embed x by (fⱼ(x)) in the cube and ∞ by zero. The image plus zero is compact: a sequence staying infinitely often in one exhaustion compact has a convergent subsequence; otherwise extract one escaping all compacts, whose coordinates tend to zero. The cube metric makes X=E∪{∞} compact metrizable. Coordinates recover E’s topology; neighborhoods of ∞ contain complements of compact sets. For compact E, ∞ is isolated, with the same convention.

## 2. From Hausdorff Distance to Fell Topology

<a id="fell-polish"></a>

For nonempty compact subsets of X define \(d_H(A,B)=\max\{\sup_{a\in A}r(a,B),\sup_{b\in B}r(b,A)\}\), with r the compactification metric. It is a metric and \(d_H(A,B)=\|r(\cdot,A)-r(\cdot,B)\|_\infty\): use the triangle inequality in one direction and test on A and B in the other.

All nonempty subsets of a finite ε-net of X form a finite ε-net for its hyperspace, proving total boundedness. For Hausdorff-Cauchy Aₙ the distance functions converge uniformly to nonnegative 1-Lipschitz g. A compact subsequence of aₙ∈Aₙ shows g has a zero. Let A={g=0}. For each x choose nearest aₙ∈Aₙ and a subsequence tending to a∈A; then g(x)=r(x,a). The Lipschitz bound gives g(x)≤r(x,A), so g is distance to A. Hence Aₙ→A. Completeness and total boundedness prove compactness and separability.

The map \(F\mapsto F\cup\{\infty\}\) bijects \(\mathcal F(E)\) with the nonempty compacts containing ∞, a Hausdorff-closed subspace, hence compact Polish. Hausdorff topology equals Vietoris topology: containment in open V is open by positive distance to its complement, and hitting V is open by a small ball at a hit point. Conversely, use a finite ε/2-net, requiring each ball to be hit and containment in the ε-neighborhood, to obtain a neighborhood inside the Hausdorff ε-ball.

In the subspace containing ∞, hitting an open set containing ∞ is automatic; remaining lower conditions hit an open U of E. Upper conditions only matter for ∞∈V, when K=X∖V is compact in E. Thus the pulled-back topology is generated by \(H_U=\{F:F\cap U\ne\varnothing\}\) and \(M_K=\{F:F\cap K=\varnothing\}\), precisely Fell topology. The empty set corresponds to {∞}.

## 3. Open and Compact Hits Generate One Structure

<a id="fell-effros"></a>

Compact hits \(H_K=M_K^c\) are Fell-closed. For \(U_n=\{x:\rho(x,K)<1/n\}\) one has \(H_K=\bigcap_nH_{U_n}\): if F hits all Uₙ, choose hit points in one compact neighborhood and take a subsequence, using closedness to reach F∩K. Compact hits are therefore in the Effros σ-algebra generated by open hits. All Fell subbasic sets belong to it, and second countability makes every open a countable union of finite subbasic intersections. Thus Fell Borel equals Effros.

Compact hits indexed by R form a countable determining family: every point of open U belongs to the interior of some K∈R contained in U, so \(H_U\) is the countable union of these \(H_K\). The reverse inclusion was proved above. Thus a random closed set requires measurability of all open-hit events, with no standard Borel assumption on the original probability space; its law is a Borel probability on the Polish hyperspace. Measurability of singleton-membership indicators alone does not supply all open hits.

## 4. Sequences and the Boundaries of Operations

<a id="fell-sequences"></a>

Fₙ→F is equivalent to: (i) every x∈F has eventually defined xₙ∈Fₙ tending to x; (ii) any convergent selection along a subsequence has its limit in F. Open hits give (i) using successive small balls. Failure of (ii) would give a compact neighborhood of the limit disjoint from F, contradicting eventual avoidance. Conversely (i) ensures required open hits; infinitely many hits of a compact that should be avoided provide a convergent subsequence violating (ii). The lower condition is vacuous for an empty limit.

Finite union is continuous: use one component for lower approximations and a fixed-component subsequence for upper limits. Intersection is not: Aₙ={0}, Bₙ={1/n} both tend to {0}, while intersections stay empty. If a:E→E is continuous and proper, a(F) is closed and F↦a(F) is continuous: preimages of convergent image sequences stay in a compact set, proving closedness and the upper condition; continuity gives the lower one. Without properness images can fail to be closed, so no unconditional continuous-mapping claim is valid.

## 5. Local Compactness Cannot Be Silently Removed

<a id="fell-nonlocal"></a>

In an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space, the same hit-open/miss-compact topology cannot separate ∅ and {0}. A basic neighborhood of ∅ misses compact K; one of {0} hits finitely many open Uᵢ containing zero and misses finitely many compact Lⱼ not containing zero. A small ball inside their intersection cannot lie in compact K∪⋃Lⱼ, since zero would then have a compact neighborhood. Choose y in that ball outside the union; {y} lies in both neighborhoods. This Fell topology is not Hausdorff; our compact-Polish theorem does not extend to general Polish spaces.
