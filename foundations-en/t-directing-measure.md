# Random Directing Probabilities, Standard Borel Structure and de Finetti

## 1. Making a probability a measurable parameter

<a id="directing-proof-space"></a>

Let E be nonempty standard Borel. Give the set P(E) of countably additive probabilities the evaluation σ-field generated by μ↦μ(A). A countable generating algebra containing E suffices: the sets whose evaluations are measurable form a Dynkin class containing the algebra. The same algebra determines probabilities by π–λ.

We also prove that this parameter space is standard Borel, allowing its laws and posterior kernels. Use the Borel isomorphism of Chapter10 from E onto B⊂[0,1]. Encode probabilities on [0,1] by their CDF values at rational q. The following countably specified constraints define a Borel subset C of a countable cube.

\[ 0\le F_q\le1,\quad F_q\le F_r\ (q<r),\quad F_q=\inf_{\substack{r>q\\r\in\mathbb Q}}F_r,\quad F_q=0\ (q<0),\quad F_q=1\ (q\ge1). \]

For a valid code F and 0<u<1, let Q(F,u) be the infimum of rational q with Fq≥u. It lies in [0,1] and is jointly measurable, since {Q<a} is the union of {u≤Fq} over rational q<a. Right continuity makes its CDF exactly F: for rational q, {Q≤q} is the intersection of {U≤Fr} over r>q. Each code therefore decodes to an actual probability, not merely finitely consistent numbers.

\[ \mu_F(A)=\int_0^1\mathbf1_A(Q(F,u))\,du,\qquad \mathcal C_B=\{F\in\mathcal C:\mu_F(B)=1\}. \]

For every Borel A the integral is measurable in F, so Cᴮ is Borel. Decode Q outside B as a fixed e₀; for every F∈Cᴮ this changes only a null u-section. This gives a measurable bijection with measurable inverse between Cᴮ and P(E). Standard Borelness and evaluation measurability are proved without Chapter49.

## 2. A conditional law for an arbitrary information σ-field

<a id="directing-proof-conditional"></a>

The exact Chapter10 result needed here has standard Borel target E but an arbitrary conditioning sub-σ-field H. No standard Borel quotient of H is assumed. This is not a claim of regular conditional probabilities for arbitrary target spaces.

For Y=b(X), choose versions of E[1{Y≤q}|H] at rational q. Outside a countable common null set they are ordered, between zero and one, and have the correct boundary values. Conditional monotone convergence along a chosen rational sequence decreasing to each rational q gives right continuity; countably many sequences suffice. Replace bad H-measurable points by the code of δb(e₀). The decoding above gives a probability kernel on [0,1].

Verify the conditional integral identity on rational half-lines and extend by a monotone class. Since Y∈B, the kernel assigns mass one to B almost surely. Replace the kernel on this single bad set by δb(e₀), then push back by b⁻¹. It is a probability at every sample point. Conditional identities for uncountably many tests are interpreted separately modulo null sets, while countable additivity holds pointwise for the kernel.

The same result applies to the entire record X=(X₁,X₂,…), since its countable product is standard Borel. The conditional law of the whole record given J therefore exists. No future jointly parameterized disintegration theorem is presumed.

## 3. From decreasing information to conditional products

<a id="directing-proof-products"></a>

On the infinite exchangeable standard Borel record space, let ν be the conditional law of X₁ given J. It is a J-measurable P(E)-valued variable. For every member of a countable generating algebra A, finite conditional averaging and the reverse martingale theorem give empirical convergence to its ν-evaluation. Take a common null set for this countable class.

\[ L_N(A)=E[\mathbf1_A(X_1)\mid\mathcal G_N]\longrightarrow E[\mathbf1_A(X_1)\mid\mathcal J]=\nu(A)\qquad(A\in\mathcal A). \]

For fixed k and a k-tuple from A, the preceding companion proved that the conditional rectangle probability given Gₙ is a without-replacement average, differing from the product of empirical evaluations by at most c. The reverse martingale theorem identifies the left limit, and empirical convergence identifies the right one. Word lengths and algebra tuples still form a countable family.

\[ E\!\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^k\mathbf1_{A_\ell}(X_\ell)\,\middle|\,\mathcal J\right]=\prod_{\ell=1}^k\nu(A_\ell). \]

The order is crucial: conditional laws first establish countable additivity of ν, and empirical averages then identify it. We do not assign limits to all sets and simply declare the result a probability.

## 4. A kernel for the whole record and common null sets

<a id="directing-proof-path"></a>

For each μ∈P(E), Chapters8 or10 construct the unique infinite product. This is a probability kernel in μ: cylinder evaluations are finite products of μ-evaluations, and a Dynkin-class argument extends measurability to all path events. Denote this kernel by K(μ,·).

\[ K(\mu,A_1\times\cdots\times A_k\times E\times\cdots)=\prod_{\ell=1}^k\mu(A_\ell),\qquad K(\mu,\cdot)=\mu^{\otimes\mathbb N}. \]

Let Rω be the regular conditional law of the whole record given J. The preceding identities make it agree with K(ν(ω),·) on all countable algebra cylinders. Outside their common J-null set, both are probabilities agreeing on a determining class, hence are equal as path measures by π–λ. Thus K(ν,·) itself is a conditional path kernel given J.

\[ P(X\in D\mid\mathcal J)=K(\nu,D),\qquad P(X\in D\mid\sigma(\nu))=K(\nu,D)\quad(D\in\mathcal E^{\otimes\mathbb N}). \]

The second identity follows by the tower property and σ(ν)⊂J. Thus the coordinates are conditionally iid with law ν. Integrating over its distribution M gives the mixture. Conversely any such mixture is exchangeable, since each product kernel is invariant under finite permutations.

\[ P(X\in D)=\int_{\mathcal P(E)}\mu^{\otimes\mathbb N}(D)\,M(d\mu),\qquad M=\mathcal L(\nu). \]

For a parameter event B and record event D, the same conditional integration gives P(ν∈B,X∈D)=∫B K(μ,D)M(dμ). Rectangles determine the joint law by π–λ. Thus later jointly measurable events depending on μ, such as agreement of the empirical limit with that parameter, may also be integrated using this kernel.

The common null set is for the countable determining class used to identify two measures. It does not assert simultaneous pointwise conditional identities for every sample-dependent event. Those quantifiers cannot be interchanged.

## 5. A tail-measurable directing version

<a id="directing-proof-tail"></a>

For encoded observations Y=b(X), take the limsup empirical CDF at each rational q. Deleting a finite prefix changes the average by a vanishing amount, so these readings are measurable for the raw tail σ-field T. On a probability-one set they equal the CDF code of ν and hence satisfy the valid-code and B-support conditions.

Code validity is Borel, so invalid tail codes can uniformly be replaced by δe₀. This gives a genuinely T-measurable P(E)-valued νᵀ equal to ν almost surely. Replacing ν by it preserves conditional iid. Repairing one countable code avoids destroying additivity by separately modifying uncountably many evaluations.

## 6. Uniqueness of the directing probability and its mixing law

<a id="directing-proof-unique"></a>

If Λ on the same space also makes the record conditionally iid(Λ), the strong law holds simultaneously on the countable determining algebra under each fixed μ. The event is jointly measurable in (μ,record). Conditional-kernel integration gives Lₙ(A)→Λ(A) simultaneously there. Our construction already gives limits ν(A), so Λ=ν almost surely as probabilities.

For an alternative mixing distribution M′, construct M′(dμ)K(μ,dx) on P(E)×Eᴺ. The directing probability extracted from the record equals μ, so its distribution is M′. But it is a measurable function of the record whose law P already determines M. Hence M′=M. An arbitrary parameter θ need not be identifiable; the resulting probability μ is unique, not every parameter label.

The representation uses the actual kernels, strong law and reverse martingales of Chapters8–12 and15, plus the probability parameter space constructed here. It does not borrow Chapters44,45,47 or49. For a record on another sample space, push to the canonical record space and pull the directing function back. No claim is made about extraneous information outside the record.
